## During model: which counties stay dark longest

Target: hours from event start to stop with at least 10% of the county's customers out. One row per event × county of the event's primary BA. Features are known at the event start. Baseline: the county's mean dark hours in its past events.

## v1 (for the record)

v1 used train < 2021 and test 2021-2024 (818 events), with v1 features. `warn_full` warned 114 events at least 12 h ahead (13.9%) at 6.34 false alarms per BA-year; `rule_forecast_error` warned 60 at 6.09 and `rule_hand` 76 at 6.92. So v1 warn_full did not beat both baselines at the same false-alarm rate. It missed Texas 2021 and TVA Elliott. The v1 dark model (L2 on hours) had test MAE 3.91 h against 3.78 h for the history baseline (worse), and within-event Spearman 0.313 against 0.216.

## Limits

- Recall at 12 h stays low. Most outage events are distribution-level storm damage that does not show in BA demand data.
- On DEV, every method went above the 6 false alarms per BA-year it was held to on TRAIN. Positive hours are more common in later years, so a threshold chosen on TRAIN alerts more often later.
- `warn_fcst` uses observed future weather as its forecast. A real forecast has errors, so its numbers are an upper bound.
- `warn_full` uses outages already reported (lagged 1 h); the live page cannot compute these. `warn_grid` uses the BA's own demand records, which the live page needs a long history to compute.
- The 72 h trailing summaries use the last 72 rows of each BA. That equals 72 h only where the hourly table has no gaps.
- DEV was used to choose the design after v1 had shown it. Only the FINAL TEST is a clean hold-out.
- SVI is the 2022 release for the equity table. Events are defined by a share-of-BA-customers rule on EAGLE-I data.
